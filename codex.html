The Chush Codex

volume i. for those who have stopped guessing.

§0 · On being here

If you are reading this, you were told the book had a name and you trusted it. That was correct. The word for that door is archive.

Nothing in this book is a hint. Everything in this book is an answer, if you can prove it.

§1 · On doors

Every door opens with one word. Only its letters count. Spaces, capitals and punctuation are ignored. The word is spoken after the # in the address, and nowhere else.

Some doors show a mark in the corner of what they say. The mark names one of the ciphers below. A door with no mark expects you to recognise the cipher yourself.

§2 · The ciphers

M · the mirror
The alphabet folded in half. The first letter is the last, the second is the second-to-last.
S · the slip
The writer's hand slid one key to the right on the three rows of letters of an ordinary keyboard. At the end of a row, the hand fell back to its start.
P · the old phone
Press a key once for its first letter, twice for its second. Each letter is one group.
L · the ladder
Written down and up across three rungs, then read rung by rung, top to bottom.
T · the turn
Read it backwards, then move every letter thirteen places.
N · the count
Each letter by its place in the alphabet, plus the number of the door you stand at. In the ledger, you stand at the ledger's own number.
Q · the square
Row, then column, in the square below.
K · the key
Each letter moved forward by the matching letter of a key (a moves nothing, b moves one), the key repeating as often as it must. The door tells you which key, never what it is.
D · the door's turn
Every letter moved forward by the number of the door, wrapping around.
G · the shapes
Each letter is a pattern of nine dots. See §3.
F · the faces
Each letter has a face. See §4.
B · the book
Pairs of numbers: paragraph, then word, in §5. Take the first letter of each word.

§5 · The book of static

1. Before the doors there was only static, and the static was patient. It hummed under every quiet room and waited for someone to listen closely enough to hear a voice inside it.

2. Keepers came and went. Each one left a lantern, a ledger, or a lie. Nobody kept all three. The xylophone in the cellar still plays one note when the wind is wrong, and nobody knows who tuned it.

3. Under the floorboards there is a map of every hallway, drawn in zinc and yellow wax. It shows rooms that were never built and omits the one you are standing in. Quietly, the map updates itself.

4. Venture far enough and the walls begin to whisper numbers. Write them down. Ignore the ones that rhyme. Gather the rest into a jar, and the jar will tell you which door is honest.

5. Every answer you have given is remembered. Even the wrong ones are filed away, ordered by how sure you sounded. Do not apologise. Doors do not care about apologies, only about keys.

6. In the end the static will ask you one question. It is not difficult. It is only the oldest question in the building, and you answered it once already, at the very beginning.

§6 · The ledger

Each line holds the true word for one door, hidden among liars, all written in the same cipher. To know which is true, weigh each word: multiply every letter's place in the alphabet by its own position in the word, and add them all. The door tells you the weight.
